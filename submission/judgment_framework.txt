JUDGMENT FRAMEWORK — THREE QUESTIONS FOR EACH PART

Part | What can go wrong? | What does the system do? | What must a human check?
Input CSV | Bad date, duplicate ID, broken comma quoting | Validate required columns, IDs and date format; Python csv module reads quoted fields | Confirm submitted claim record is complete
LLM extraction | Hallucinated amount/category/bill; prompt injection | Fixed system prompt, JSON mode, schema validation and local fallback | Review unclear notes and unusual categories
Amount fact check | LLM calculates or selects an unrelated number | Compare amount to numbers_in(note); no multiplication or word-to-number conversion | Resolve notes containing multiple plausible totals
Bill check | Claim says bill is coming later or absent | Python scans bill/receipt/invoice/ticket wording and missing/later phrases | Open and inspect the actual original document
Split-claim check | Large request divided into smaller same-day claims | Group by (club, category, date); flag combined amount > ₹5,000 | Decide whether the claims are genuinely related
Budget rules | A rejected claim accidentally consumes budget | Only ok_to_pay updates tuple-key running totals | Confirm budget limits are current
Reports | Wrong row exported or date lost | Write results, payable, review and summary; retain original note | Treasurer signs each payment after bill inspection

Wrongly marked OK vs wrongly sent for review
A claim wrongly marked OK is the more serious failure: money could be paid without adequate evidence or
outside policy. A claim wrongly sent for review causes delay and extra work but can be corrected by a person.
Therefore the system uses conservative extraction and fails toward review when facts are unclear.

Human checkpoint
The faculty treasurer signs every payment only after seeing the original bill. The application itself never
pays, approves, or authorizes a transaction.
